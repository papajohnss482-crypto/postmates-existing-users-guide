Postmates Promo Codes for Existing Users: What Actually Works in October 2026

Published October 20, 2026. Offer evidence on this page was checked inside the Postmates app on October 2, 2026 and re-checked on October 12, 2026. Where a finding comes from community reports instead of the app, it is labeled that way.

Quick answer. Existing users are not locked out of saving, but the savings rarely come from a public code typed in at checkout. In October 2026 the working routes are $12 Meal Deals, Uber One, and promotions targeted to your own account inside the app. Public codes are mostly new user offers or one-time targeted offers, which is why they fail for everyone else.

Why existing users see so few codes

Postmates, owned by Uber, runs most of its discounting through targeting rather than public codes. An offer can be tied to one account, one city, one restaurant group, or one order history pattern. When a code like that gets shared on a coupon site or a forum, it spreads far beyond the accounts it was made for, and nearly every person who tries it sees an error. The code was real. It was just never theirs.

This is also why lists of twenty or thirty Postmates codes are misleading. At the time of our October checks, the app itself showed no standalone free delivery codes at all. The real offers lived in structured programs, listed in the table below.

How to check a code properly in the app

  1. Open the Postmates app and go to your account.
  2. Open Promotions, then tap Add promo code.
  3. Type the code exactly as written, codes are not case sensitive in most cases, but spacing matters if you paste it.
  4. Read the confirmation. If the app accepts the code, it shows the discount terms, the minimum order, and the expiry that applies to your account. If it rejects the code, it was not issued for your account, and no other site can change that.

Checking this way costs nothing and places no order. It is also the only real test. A code that a website calls tested or verified still fails if it was targeted at someone else.

Why the expiry dates on coupon sites are wrong

Our tracking found a repeat pattern: listing pages extend their own expiry countdowns. A code shown as ending in 8 days can quietly become 11 days a short time later, with nothing about the offer itself changing. Those countdowns are marketing timers on the listing page, not deadlines from Postmates. Treat every expiry printed on a listing site as per community reports, unverified, and trust only the date the app shows after a code is accepted on your account.

The opposite pattern is also real. When a code is delisted from its source on its stated expiry day, it is done. In our own tracking, delisted codes are dropped from the list, never quietly kept alive with a new date.

Grocery orders versus restaurant orders

Existing users generally find more structured value on restaurant orders, through Meal Deals and Uber One fee savings, than on grocery orders. Grocery promotions in the app tend to be store specific and short lived, and in our October checks they changed less predictably than the restaurant programs. If you order groceries weekly, the reliable saving is the fee side of Uber One, covered on the Uber One value page, rather than hunting for a grocery code that may be tied to one store in one city.

Our research method, in short

Every claim in this report is dated. Offers marked verified in-app come from screens inside the Postmates app captured on the dates shown. Offers marked community-reported come from public user reports and are labeled unverified.
